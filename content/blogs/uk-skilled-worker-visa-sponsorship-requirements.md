## What is a UK Sponsor Licence?

Before you can hire someone on a Skilled Worker visa, your business must hold a valid UK sponsor licence. This licence is essentially the Home Office granting your company permission to hire foreign workers.

To get a sponsor licence, a business must prove that it is a legitimate, trading entity in the UK. The Home Office will want to see that your business is capable of offering genuine employment that meets the required skill and salary levels.

**The basic requirements for a sponsor licence include:**
*   Having no unspent criminal convictions for immigration offenses or certain other crimes.
*   Having no history of failing to carry out your sponsorship duties.
*   Having appropriate HR systems in place to monitor sponsored employees.
*   Paying the application fee (currently [VERIFY: current fee, e.g., £536] for small/charitable sponsors and [VERIFY: current fee, e.g., £1,476] for medium/large sponsors).

Once granted, a sponsor licence is valid for **ten years** (extended from four years following an April 2024 rule change). It remains valid as long as you continue meeting your sponsor duties — no routine renewal is required.

## What is a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)?

Once your business has a sponsor licence, the next step in fulfilling the UK skilled worker visa sponsorship requirements is assigning a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) to your chosen candidate.

A CoS is not a physical paper certificate. It is an electronic record generated in the Home Office's Sponsor Management System (SMS). Every CoS has a unique reference number, which the employee will need to apply for their visa.

**There are two types of CoS:**
1.  **Defined CoS:** You must apply for a Defined CoS if you are hiring someone who is currently *outside* the UK and applying for their entry clearance visa.
2.  **Undefined CoS:** You use an Undefined CoS if you are hiring someone who is already *inside* the UK and switching from another visa (like a Student visa or Graduate visa) to a Skilled Worker visa.

When you assign a CoS, you are officially confirming that you will sponsor this individual and that the job offer is genuine.

## What Are the Job and Salary Requirements?

You cannot sponsor a worker for just any job. The Home Office has strict rules about the types of roles that qualify for sponsorship.

Before assigning a CoS, the employer must ensure the role meets these two key criteria:

### 1. Skill Level
The job must be on the Home Office's list of eligible occupations. Currently, the minimum skill level is RQF Level 3 (which is roughly equivalent to A-levels). This means many administrative, retail, and hospitality roles do not qualify for sponsorship, while most professional, technical, and managerial roles do.

### 2. Salary Thresholds

However, the employer must also check the specific "going rate" for the occupation code. The employee must be paid whichever amount is higher.

*Note: There are discounts to this salary threshold if the candidate is under 26, switching from a Student visa (a "New Entrant"), or has a PhD relevant to the job.*

## What Are Your Tier 2 Sponsor Duties?

Holding a sponsor licence is a privilege, and the Home Office expects employers to act as their "eyes and ears" in the workplace. If you fail to meet your Tier 2 sponsor duties, your licence can be downgraded, suspended, or completely revoked.

**As a sponsor, you must:**
*   **Keep records:** You must keep copies of the employee's passport, visa, National Insurance number, and contact details.
*   **Monitor attendance:** You must report to the Home Office if the sponsored worker is absent from work without permission for more than 10 consecutive working days.
*   **Report changes:** You must inform the Home Office if the worker's contract ends early, if they resign, or if there are significant changes to their job title, duties, or salary.
*   **Comply with the law:** You must ensure you are paying the correct salary, abiding by UK employment law, and only employing people who have the legal right to work in the UK.

## Common Mistakes Employers Make

Navigating the UK skilled worker visa sponsorship requirements can be tricky. Avoid these costly mistakes:

*   **Assigning the wrong type of CoS:** If you assign an Undefined CoS to someone applying from outside the UK, their visa application will be refused.
*   **Failing to pay the Immigration Skills Charge (ISC):** Unless an exemption applies (like switching from a Student visa), employers must pay the ISC when assigning a CoS. Forgetting this payment will delay the visa process.
*   **Not keeping HR systems updated:** The Home Office can conduct unannounced compliance visits. If your employee records are messy or incomplete, you risk losing your licence.
*   **Calculating the salary incorrectly:** Salary calculations cannot include bonuses, allowances, or overtime. It must be based solely on guaranteed basic gross pay.

(The visa application fee and health surcharge are usually paid by the employee, though some employers choose to cover them.)

**Q: How long does it take to get a UK sponsor licence?**  
A: The standard processing time for a sponsor licence application is usually around 8 weeks. However, you can often pay an additional fee for priority service to get a decision within 10 working days.

**Q: Does the employer have to advertise the job first?**  
A: Under the current rules, the strict "Resident Labour Market Test" (which required employers to advertise roles locally for 28 days) has been abolished. However, you still must prove the job is a genuine vacancy and not created just to secure a visa for someone.
