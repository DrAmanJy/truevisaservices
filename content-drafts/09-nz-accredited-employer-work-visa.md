## What is the NZ Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)?

The NZ accredited employer work visa is the main temporary work visa for New Zealand. It is designed to ensure that businesses only hire foreign workers when there is a genuine New Zealand skills shortage that cannot be filled by local citizens or residents.

The AEWV system is "employer-led." This means that you, as the migrant worker, cannot simply apply for the visa on your own. You can only apply if an approved New Zealand employer offers you a job and supports your application.

## How Does the AEWV Application Process Work?

Because this visa is employer-led, the AEWV application process involves three distinct steps. The employer is responsible for the first two steps, and you are responsible for the final step.

Here is a step-by-step breakdown:

1.  **Employer Accreditation:** The New Zealand business must apply to Immigration New Zealand (INZ) to become an "Accredited Employer." This proves the business is genuine and treats its employees well.
2.  **The Job Check:** Once accredited, the employer must apply for a "Job Check" for the specific role they want to fill. They must prove that they advertised the role locally first (if applicable) and that the job pays at least the required minimum salary.
3.  **The Visa Application:** Once the Job Check is approved, the employer will send you a "Job Check token." You use this unique code to submit your actual visa application, proving you have the skills and experience for the job.

## What Are the Key Requirements for Applicants?

Securing a job offer is the biggest hurdle, but you still must prove that you are eligible for the visa. The main New Zealand work visa requirements for the AEWV include:

### 1. Meeting the Skills Match
You must prove that you actually have the skills and experience required for the specific job you were offered. If the employer's Job Check states the role requires three years of experience and a specific degree, you must provide the evidence (like reference letters and educational certificates) to prove you meet those exact standards.

### 2. Earning the Median Wage
In most cases, your job offer must pay at least the current New Zealand median wage. Currently, the minimum hourly rate is [VERIFY: current wage, e.g., $29.66 NZD per hour]. 

*Note: There are some exceptions to this rule. Certain roles in the tourism, hospitality, and care work sectors have special agreements that allow employers to pay slightly below the median wage.*

### 3. Health and Character Checks
Like most countries, New Zealand requires you to prove you are of good character (by providing police certificates from countries you have lived in) and in good health (by undergoing an approved medical examination).

## Finding an Accredited Employer NZ

Because you cannot apply for the AEWV without a sponsor, finding an accredited employer NZ is your top priority. You cannot work for just any company—they must be officially registered with INZ.

**Here is how you can find these employers:**
*   **Search official databases:** While INZ does not publicly list every accredited employer to protect business privacy, many large recruitment agencies in New Zealand have lists of approved companies.
*   **Look for the label:** When searching on New Zealand job boards like Seek or TradeMe, many employers will specifically state "Accredited Employer" in their job advertisements.
*   **Target specific shortages:** Focus your job hunt on industries facing a severe New Zealand skills shortage, such as healthcare, construction, engineering, and IT. These companies are almost always accredited because they rely heavily on international talent.

## Can the AEWV Lead to Permanent Residency?

Yes! The AEWV is a temporary work visa, but it serves as the main stepping stone to securing a New Zealand resident visa (which is their version of permanent residency).

If your job is on the "Green List" (a list of highly skilled, in-demand occupations), you might be eligible for the **Straight to Residence** or **Work to Residence** pathways. 

Even if your job is not on the Green List, working in New Zealand on an AEWV allows you to claim points toward the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) resident visa, providing a clear route to staying in the country permanently.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

A simple mistake during the AEWV process can cause months of delays. Avoid these common traps:

*   **Applying without the Job Check token:** You cannot start your visa application until your employer officially gives you their approved Job Check token number.
*   **Providing weak work references:** INZ is very strict about checking your work history. Your reference letters must be on official company letterhead and list your exact duties and dates of employment. 
*   **Forgetting translation rules:** All documents you submit must be in English. If your police certificates or degrees are in another language, you must use an approved, certified translator.
*   **Quitting your job too early:** Your AEWV is tied to your specific employer. If you quit or are fired, you cannot simply start working for another company. You must apply for a "Variation of Conditions" or a brand new visa with a new accredited employer.
