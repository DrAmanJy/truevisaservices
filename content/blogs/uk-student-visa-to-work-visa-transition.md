# UK Student Visa to Work Visa: The Complete Transition Guide

Graduating from a UK university is an incredible achievement. However, for many international students, the excitement of graduation is quickly replaced by a stressful new challenge: figuring out how to stay and work in the country legally. The clock is ticking on your student visa, and the rules around UK work visa sponsorship can feel overwhelming.

If you are worried about packing your bags and leaving the career opportunities of the UK behind, take a deep breath. With the right strategy, switching from a student visa to a work visa is entirely possible. At True Visa, we have helped thousands of international graduates successfully secure their employment rights in the UK. 

In this guide, we will break down the UK student visa to work visa transition step-by-step. You will learn about your two main options—the Graduate Route and the Skilled Worker visa—the exact requirements for each, and the most common mistakes that cause applications to fail. 

## What Are Your Options for Staying in the UK After Graduation?

When your Student Visa (formerly known as the Tier 4 visa) expires, you cannot simply stay in the UK to look for work. You must switch to a new visa category before your current visa ends.

For most international graduates, there are two primary pathways to remain in the UK and build a career:
1.  **The Graduate Route Visa:** A short-term, flexible visa that lets you work without needing a sponsor.
2.  **The Skilled Worker Visa:** A long-term visa that requires a specific job offer from an approved UK employer.

Both pathways have unique benefits, and the right choice depends on whether you already have a job offer and what your long-term goals are in the UK.

## How Does the UK Graduate Route Visa Work?

The UK post study work visa, officially called the Graduate Route, is often the easiest first step for recent graduates. This visa gives you permission to stay in the UK for at least two years (or three years if you hold a PhD) after successfully completing your course.

**Key Benefits of the Graduate Route:**
*   **No Sponsor Needed:** You do not need a job offer to apply. You can spend your time attending interviews and searching for the perfect role.
*   **Total Flexibility:** You can work in almost any job, at any salary level. You can even be self-employed or work freelance.
*   **Easy Application:** You simply apply online from inside the UK before your student visa expires.

**The Requirements:**
*   You must be in the UK when you apply.
*   Your university must notify the Home Office that you have successfully completed your degree.
*   You must pay the application fee of [VERIFY: current fee, e.g., £822] and the Immigration Health Surcharge of [VERIFY: current IHS fee, e.g., £1,035 per year].

*Note: Time spent on the Graduate Route does not count directly toward the 5-year route to Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR), which is the UK's version of permanent residency.*

## How Do You Switch to a Skilled Worker Visa?

If you want a direct pathway to permanent residency, or if your Graduate visa is expiring, you will need to switch to a Skilled Worker visa. This is the main work visa for the UK, and it allows you to stay for up to five years before applying to settle permanently.

However, getting a Skilled Worker visa requires a bit more preparation than the Graduate Route.

**To qualify, you must meet these strict requirements:**
1.  **Approved Employer:** You must have a confirmed job offer from an employer who holds a valid UK sponsor licence.
2.  **Skill Level:** Your job must be on the list of eligible occupations (generally at the RQF Level 3, which is A-level equivalent or higher).
3.  **Salary Threshold:** You must be paid a minimum salary. Currently, the standard threshold is [VERIFY: current minimum salary, e.g., £38,700 per year], or the "going rate" for your specific job—whichever is higher. 
4.  **English Language:** You usually meet this requirement automatically by having a degree from a UK university.

### The "New Entrant" Salary Discount
If the high salary threshold worries you, there is good news. As a recent student or someone under the age of 26, you may qualify as a "New Entrant." This allows your employer to sponsor you at a discounted salary rate—typically 70% to 90% of the standard going rate—making it much easier for companies to hire you as a junior employee.

## Should I Choose the Graduate Route or the Skilled Worker Visa?

This is the most common question we hear from students. Here is a simple breakdown to help you decide.

**Choose the Graduate Route if:**
*   You do not have a job offer yet.
*   Your current job offer is from a company that does not have a sponsor licence.
*   The salary you are being offered is too low for the Skilled Worker visa.
*   You want to try working in a few different industries before settling down.

**Choose the Skilled Worker Visa if:**
*   You have a job offer from an approved sponsor that meets the salary rules.
*   Your main goal is to get Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) as fast as possible.
*   You want your employer to cover your visa fees (many corporate sponsors pay these costs for their employees).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid During Your Transition

Transitioning your visa status requires careful timing. Avoid these common errors that cause students to lose their right to stay in the UK:

*   **Leaving the UK while your application is pending:** If you travel outside the Common Travel Area (UK, Ireland, Channel Islands, and Isle of Man) while your Graduate or Skilled Worker visa application is processing, your application will be automatically withdrawn.
*   **Applying before your university confirms your graduation:** For the Graduate visa, you must wait until your university officially tells the Home Office you have passed your course. Do not apply just because you finished your final exams.
*   **Forgetting about the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS):** The IHS is a mandatory upfront cost. If you are applying for a 3-year Skilled Worker visa, you must pay three years of the IHS at once, which can cost thousands of pounds.
*   **Working a permanent job too early:** While waiting for your Graduate visa, you can only work within the limits of your Student visa (usually 20 hours a week). You cannot start a full-time, permanent contract until your new visa is actually approved.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**Q: Can I apply for the Graduate visa from my home country?**  
A: No. You must be physically present in the UK, holding a valid Student visa, at the time you submit your Graduate visa application. 

**Q: Can I switch from a Graduate visa to a Skilled Worker visa later?**  
A: Yes! This is a very common strategy. Many students use the Graduate visa to gain two years of UK work experience, prove their value to an employer, and then ask that employer for UK work visa sponsorship before the Graduate visa expires.

**Q: Does my employer have to pay a fee to sponsor me?**  
A: Yes. Employers must pay the Immigration Skills Charge (ISC) to sponsor a Skilled Worker. This costs [VERIFY: current fee, e.g., £1,000 per year] for medium/large companies, or [VERIFY: current fee, e.g., £364 per year] for small charities and businesses. However, if you are switching directly from a Student visa to a Skilled Worker visa in the UK, your employer is exempt from paying this specific charge!
