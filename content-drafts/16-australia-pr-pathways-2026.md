## How Does the Australian Skilled Migration System Work?

Before comparing the specific visas, you must understand the basic framework of Australia's skilled migration program, known as SkillSelect.

All three of these visas (189, 190, and 491) are points-tested. This means the Australian government ranks your profile based on factors like your age, English language skills, education, and work experience. To be eligible to apply, you must score a minimum of [VERIFY: current points threshold, e.g., 65 points]. 

You must first submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) through the SkillSelect system. Once you submit your EOI, you wait to receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA) from the government. You cannot apply for these visas until you receive an official invitation.

## What is the Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189)?

The Subclass 189 visa is often considered the "gold standard" of Australia permanent residency. It is a federal visa, meaning it is managed directly by the Australian national government.

This visa is designed for highly skilled workers whose occupations are in extreme demand across the entire country. You do not need a sponsor, an employer, or a state government to nominate you.

**Key Benefits:**
*   You are granted full Permanent Residency immediately.
*   You can live, work, and study anywhere in Australia from day one.
*   You have immediate access to Medicare (Australia's public healthcare system).
*   You can sponsor eligible family members to join you.

**Is it right for you?**
The 189 visa is highly competitive. Because it offers total freedom, the government only invites candidates with the absolute highest points. Your occupation must be on the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL). If your score is simply at the minimum threshold (e.g., 65 points), it is very unlikely you will receive an invite for a 189 visa.

## What is the State Sponsored Visa (Subclass 190)?

The Subclass 190 visa is also a permanent residency visa. However, it requires a nomination from an Australian State or Territory government (like New South Wales, Victoria, or Queensland). 

Each state has its own economy and faces different skill shortages. Therefore, each state maintains its own occupation list and sets its own specific criteria for applicants. 

**Key Benefits:**
*   You receive an extra 5 points toward your total score just for getting the state nomination.
*   It grants full Permanent Residency immediately.
*   The occupation lists are often much broader than the federal 189 list.

**The Catch:**
When a state nominates you, they are doing so because they need your skills to boost their local economy. In return, you must sign an agreement committing to live and work in that specific nominating state for your first [VERIFY: current commitment period, e.g., 2 years] in Australia.

## What is the Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa (Subclass 491)?

The Subclass 491 visa is slightly different. It is a **provisional** visa, not an immediate permanent residency visa. It allows you to live, work, and study in a designated regional area of Australia for up to 5 years. 

To get this visa, you must be nominated by a state government or sponsored by an eligible relative who already lives in a regional area. 

**Key Benefits:**
*   You receive a massive boost of 15 extra points toward your total score, making it much easier to qualify.
*   It provides a clear, guaranteed pathway to permanent residency (via the Subclass 191 visa) after you meet the regional living and working requirements.
*   Regional areas often have a lower cost of living and strong community support.

**The Catch:**
You and your family must live, work, and study strictly in a designated regional area. Major cities like Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane are generally excluded. You must also earn a minimum income of [VERIFY: current income threshold, e.g., $53,900 AUD] per year for at least three years while on this visa to qualify for full PR later.

    *   491: Provisional (leads to PR).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Applying for an Australian skilled visa is expensive and time-consuming. Avoid these common errors:

*   **Claiming points you cannot prove:** Do not claim points for work experience if you do not have the payslips, tax records, and reference letters to back it up. If you cannot prove your points when invited, your visa will be refused.
*   **Applying for the wrong occupation:** Your skills must be formally assessed by a relevant Australian assessing authority (like Engineers Australia or the ACS). If they reject your skills assessment, you cannot apply.
*   **Ignoring the 491 visa:** Many applicants only want the 189 or 190 visa and refuse to consider regional areas. By ignoring the 491 visa, you are missing out on 15 extra points and a much higher chance of being invited quickly.
*   **Letting your English test expire:** Tests like IELTS or PTE are usually only valid for [VERIFY: valid period, e.g., 3 years] for Australian immigration purposes. Ensure they are valid at the time of invitation.
